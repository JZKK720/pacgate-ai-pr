## 4. Findings from this audit

### 4.1 O23 (matter-delete orphaning) — verified fixed, and the orphans do not recur

The claim "matter delete leaves the files behind" was tested directly rather than
read from a defect record:

1. Created a matter, created its directory and a file inside it, issued
   `DELETE /api/matters/:id`.
2. Directory afterwards: **gone** (1 -> 0).

The stack also carries **21 historical orphan matter directories** (42 dirs vs 22
live matters, tenant `f79bee29`). To determine whether the current code still
produces them, a before/after experiment was run around a gate that creates and
deletes a matter (`test-sanitizer-e2e`):

| | orphans |
| --- | --- |
| before the gate | 21 |
| after the gate | 21 |
| **newly created** | **0** |

So the 21 are residue from pre-fix runs, not an ongoing leak. They are left in place
(they are the only copies of those test documents) and recorded here rather than
silently deleted.

### 4.2 No compose declares a healthcheck — 7 of 8 first-party services are unmonitored

`grep healthcheck` over `compose.prod.yaml`, `compose.bundle.yaml` and
`compose.qm.yaml` returns **nothing**. The `healthy` state that `smoke-full-stack.ps1`
reports for openviking comes from the **image** (`CMD openviking-entrypoint
--healthcheck`), not from compose.

| container | healthcheck |
| --- | --- |
| openviking | yes (from the image) |
| **pacgate-api, pacgate-db, pacgate-mcp, ocr-service, deer-flow, deer-flow-frontend, pacgate-nginx** | **none** |
| the 6 qm containers | none |

Consequence: `docker ps` cannot show that the API, MCP or OCR lanes are alive, and
`restart: unless-stopped` restarts a container that is *running* but not one that is
*wedged*. Every gate in §2 therefore has to probe behaviour instead of trusting
container state - which is why they do.

This is a real gap with a concrete cost, and it is **not fixed here**: adding
healthchecks is a compose change with restart-semantics consequences on a client
site, and it belongs in its own reviewed change.

### 4.3 Cloud models emit a separate `reasoning` field that consumes the token budget

Measured on `deepseek-v4.1-flash:cloud`:

| `max_tokens` | `finish_reason` | `content` | `reasoning` length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 | `length` | **`''` (empty)** | 47 |
| 24 | `stop` | `PONG` | 38 |
| 64 | `stop` | `PONG` | 63 |

A caller that sets a tight token budget gets a **well-formed, HTTP 200, semantically
empty** answer, with the content replaced by reasoning. The product is **not
exposed**: the configured budgets are 16384 / 8192 / 4096 per tier, far above the
truncation zone, and the cloud model was confirmed working end to end (9.9 s,
`reply='PONG'`). Recorded as a trap: it will bite any future short-budget caller
(health probes, title generation, classification).

## 5. Honest summary

- **0.1.29 does carry every fix from 0.1.25 forward** — 54 of 54 commits are
  ancestors of the built revision, 0 reverts, and the load-bearing ones were
  re-verified against the *published* artifacts rather than the repository.
- **37/37 gates pass**, 7 of them live-stack, against the published 0.1.29.
- **22 of the 28 named surfaces are verified live** in this session, and
  **long-term memory is partially verified** (cross-day persistence proven,
  aging behaviour untested).
- **3 surfaces have no test at all**: qm-team, team users, and project/data
  sharing between users. One (`skills`) is not a product surface. One
  (`pacgate-vectordb`) is a naming mismatch for `pacgate-db`, which is verified.
- **Two real gaps found and recorded, not fixed**: no compose healthchecks, and the
  cloud `reasoning` field's interaction with tight token budgets.
- **The audit itself surfaced one false alarm worth naming**: my first OpenViking
  probe reported the round trip FAILING at a 30 s budget. The committed gate budgets
  420 s because extraction is asynchronous; re-run with the right budget it passed at
  50 s. The service was never broken — the probe was.
